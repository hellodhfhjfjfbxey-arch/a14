Cosy Sock Materials

The yarn decides how warm, soft and long-lasting a pair of socks will be. Here is how the common fibres compare.

Row1

Merino wool

Merino is fine, soft wool that keeps feet warm even when slightly damp and resists odour, so socks can be worn more than once between washes. It suits everything from everyday wear to hiking. Look for a little nylon in the blend for strength at the heel and toe.

Row2

Alpaca

Alpaca fibre is hollow, which makes it noticeably warmer than sheep's wool for its weight, and it is silky rather than scratchy. Alpaca socks are lovely for cold houses and winter walks. They can lose shape if washed warm, so treat them gently.

Row3

Cashmere and mohair

These luxury fibres feel wonderful but wear faster underfoot.

  • Cashmere is very soft and warm; best for bed socks and lounging.
  • Mohair has a fluffy halo and traps air well.
  • Both last longer in a blend with wool and nylon.
Row4

Cotton and bamboo

Cotton and bamboo viscose are soft and breathable, and good for mild days or people who find wool itchy. They hold moisture rather than wicking it, so they feel cold once damp and are less suited to snowy walks.

Row5

Fleece and synthetics

Fleece-lined and acrylic socks are warm, cheap and easy to wash, and often come with grippy soles for slippery floors. They breathe less than wool, so feet can get sweaty, but they make good indoor slipper socks.
